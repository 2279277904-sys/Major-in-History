# Historical Research Design

## Core workflow

1. Restate the user's topic without silently changing its time, place, actors, or scale.
2. Separate the topic into period, region, institution, group, event, process, and source type.
3. Propose several research questions, distinguishing descriptive, causal, comparative, intellectual-history, social-history, and material-history questions.
4. Identify what evidence would support or weaken each question.
5. List likely primary sources, secondary scholarship, archives, editions, databases, and language requirements.
6. State the main risks: anachronism, teleology, survivorship bias, source silence, category error, translation drift, and overgeneralization.
7. Produce a research plan with scope, source order, note format, argument checkpoints, and unresolved questions.

## Rules

- Do not present a research hypothesis as an established fact.
- Distinguish a source claim, a scholar's interpretation, and the Agent's working inference.
- Do not expand the topic merely because more material is available.
- Prefer a smaller question with identifiable evidence over a sweeping unsupported narrative.
- Every important factual claim must have a source target or be marked `待核验`.
- If the period or terminology is disputed, show the competing definitions.
